What a ladder is, and what it is not

A process is usually described by naming its participants and putting them in order: these species, transformed by these agents, through these intermediates. A ladder describes the same process without any of that. Each step carries exactly one number — its power π ∈ [0,1], the fraction of the remaining distance it closes — and a ladder is a finite sequence of such steps.

Because each rung closes a fraction of the gap that remains, the residuals multiply and the composite power of a ladder is

π(L) = 1 − ∏i(1 − πi)

That is the whole construct. A rung has no identity field, and this is the formalism rather than an omission: a demonstration that identities do not matter, run on objects that carry names, would be a demonstration about the names. The implementation below enforces the same discipline — its rung type has one field.

What this notebook does not claim. The powers derived here come from a contact graph, which is itself a model. Whether they match rate constants measured in a laboratory is an empirical question no cell here bears on. The corpus is real data used so the plans have something real to run on, not evidence about metabolism.

1 · The substrate

Everything rests on a finite weighted graph with a distinguished medium vertex adjacent to every item. The floor β is the least edge weight. It is strictly positive for one reason only — a finite set of strictly positive numbers has a positive minimum — and that hypothesis is load-bearing: the weights 1/n are each positive and admit no positive lower bound, so an unbounded system has no floor.

The separation cost σ(v) is the minimum weight of a cut placing v on one side and the medium on the other. We compute it on a ball of declared radius around v, and a rung's power is derived from it: π = 1 − βr(v) / σr(v).

2 · Radius is a resolution, not a threshold

A threshold can be tuned to a desired outcome, and its value is a free parameter a reader has to take on trust. A resolution cannot: its effect is monotone and its direction is a stated consequence. Raising the radius admits more of the graph into the computation, so the derived quantity identifies more items — and the mechanism is ball overlap.

We predicted this backwards. The expectation was that a coarse radius would identify more, by analogy with a matcher where a wider neighbourhood discriminates more finely. The measurement is the reverse. In that setting the radius enlarges the description of each item; here it enlarges the domain of a minimisation that different items come to share. Enlarging a description separates; enlarging a shared minimisation merges.

3 · Intensivity, and the control that localises the failure

The multiplicative law presupposes that a rung's power does not depend on what else is in the graph. If it did, composition would depend on the order the rungs were derived in. So intensivity is a requirement, and it is tested rather than assumed.

It fails for the obvious construction. A local cut key normalised by the global floor is not intensive, because β is a minimum over every edge and one distant small edge lowers it everywhere. Both the cut key and its normaliser must be local.

Why keep the near-miss. A control that differs from the candidate in every respect shows only that some difference matters. This one differs in a single component and therefore localises the failure to the normaliser. We got it wrong first, and the control is what showed where.

4 · Order dependence is graded, not abolished

There is an easy test here that cannot fail, and it is worth naming. Permuting a fixed multiset of powers cannot change 1 − ∏(1−π), because the law is symmetric in its arguments. Every candidate passes, including deliberately extensive ones. The cell below runs that comparison and reports it as non-discriminating.

The test that can fail derives the powers sequentially, so each rung sees the graph its predecessors mutated. There, intensivity buys a large reduction in order dependence — but not exact independence, because commitment reaches inside neighbouring balls, which is exactly the case the invariance theorem does not cover.

5 · Constructing a catalytic ladder

Now the construct itself. Set the powers and watch the gap close. Two quantities are worth attention: the composite, which is what the ladder achieves end to end, and the sensitivity ∂π(L)/∂πj = P/(1−πj), which says where a marginal improvement pays.

The counter-intuitive part. Sensitivity is maximised at the highest-power rung, not the weakest. The quantity being maximised is transmission, not slack: a gain at rung j reaches the output only through ∏i≠j(1−πi), so improving a weak rung buys a large local change that the rest of the ladder discards. Is the law right?

The multiplicative law is scored against three alternatives — additive, maximum and mean — on the same generated ladders. Scoring only the favoured law would not distinguish it from the others, so all four are scored and the comparison is the test.

6 · The machine: free rules, a clock, and a refusal

Shakespeare is a language in which evaluation is measurement. Its state is the contact graph, and a monotone counter M records commitments. A rule may leave M unchanged exactly when it commits no cut — forming a rung, deriving one, and reading a composite all read boundary that is already committed, so they are free. Climbing is not: it commits once per rung.

And the construct refuses. A ladder whose declared rungs cannot reach its declared target is rejected before evaluation, commits nothing, and names its shortfall. The refusal is tight — it excludes no ladder that could have succeeded — and non-vacuous, since presentable ladders fall on both sides of it. A construct that always succeeded would classify nothing.

8 · Federated querying

The last question is whether the construct fits — whether it can be added to a query language built by other hands without breaking what is already there. HFQ is a federated plan language with six verdicts applied in a fixed order, a capability check that runs before any request is issued, and a water-filling allocator that distributes a request budget.

A ladder step is local: it reaches no source, issues no request, and demands no capability. It needed no change to the capability checker or the allocator, because both guard on is this a source access rather than enumerating the local kinds they knew about. The host's own suite of sixteen checks reads 16/16 before the change and 16/16 after.

The refusal reuses the host's blame discipline. A ladder that cannot reach its target reports starved with the predecessor field empty. That is not an omission: the input arrived intact and answered correctly, so naming it would accuse a step that did nothing wrong and send the blame walk one hop too far. A bound the host already had

HFQ bounds translation retention below by the union bound ρ ≥ 1 − Σ(1−rᵢ). That is the additive relaxation of the ladder's law. Measured on the same numbers, the additive form goes vacuous — negative, hence trivially true — on most chains of four stages or more, while the multiplicative form stays informative. The two lines of work were not written with each other in view and they meet at 1 − ∏(1−xᵢ).

9 · The corpus

Two public services with different schemas and different identifier spaces, fetched once and then frozen. The host forbids network access in its adapters by construction — its claims are properties of the compiler, and a live service can neither confirm nor refute them — so the data is real but the experiment does not depend on a service being up.

What went wrong, and stayed in the record

Three findings in this notebook came out against expectation, and each is reported as it came out rather than smoothed over.

  • Intensivity needed a local normaliser, not just a local cut key. The first construction used the global floor and failed.
  • Order independence is graded, not exact. We set out to prove exactness; the measurement says otherwise, and the reason is that commitment reaches inside neighbouring balls.
  • The radius sweep runs backwards from the predicted direction, for a reason — ball overlap — reported beside the counts.

Two defects surfaced while building the integration. A regular expression read a declared target as an extra rung, because the token power occurs inside expect power — a plan would have composed a ladder nobody wrote, which is the class of silent failure the verdict rules exist to prevent. And a corpus field came back empty on first extraction; querying the live service rather than recording a plausible zero is what caught it.
