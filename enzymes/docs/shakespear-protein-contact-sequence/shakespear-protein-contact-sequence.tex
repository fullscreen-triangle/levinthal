\begin{abstract}
Shakespeare is a receiver language whose single primitive is the cut: a
commitment that fixes boundary in a finite weighted contact graph, deposits
residue at least the floor $\flo>0$, and advances a monotone clock. The
language has one composition rule---residue chaining---which sums the boundary
a process commits. Summation is order-insensitive, and the language's own
reversal lemma turns that into a limitation: no predicate definable from
chained residue can orient a process, and none can say how much of a process a
given step accomplishes.

We add a second composition rule. A \emph{rung} carries one number, its
\emph{power} $\pow\in[0,1]$: the fraction of the remaining above-floor gap it
closes. A \emph{ladder} composes rungs multiplicatively,
$1-\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$. Where residue chaining is extensive and additive, power
is intensive and multiplicative, and the two answer different questions about
the same chain.

Three results are new. First, power need not be declared: we derive it from
the separation cost against the medium, computed on a ball of declared radius,
and we show that \emph{radius is a resolution parameter, not a threshold}.
Second, intensivity is a condition rather than a consequence---and it is
delicate. Deriving power from a local cut key is not sufficient; the
normaliser must be local too. Using the global floor breaks intensivity, and
we found this because the test was built to fail. We report the resulting
order-dependence as \emph{graded}: intensivity reduces it by an order of
magnitude and monotonically in locality, but does not abolish it under
mutation. Third, the ladder supplies the language's second refusal, discharging
the standing requirement that a construct which never refuses classifies
nothing.

We give the grammar, four typing rules, four reduction rules, and proofs of
progress, preservation, and clock monotonicity for the extended language. A
free operation is admitted only under the argument the language already uses
for its one free observation---that it individuates nothing new---and we amend
the two uniqueness claims that the extension falsifies rather than leaving
them to be discovered. Twenty-three checks run, of which
twenty-one are scored and all twenty-one pass; four are negative controls, one
is reported non-discriminating with its reason, and one records a claim the
suite cannot test.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{What the language already does, and the one thing it cannot say}

Shakespeare is a language in which evaluation is measurement
\citep{sachikonye2026shakespeare}. Its state is a finite weighted contact graph
$\Gph=(V,E,\wt)$ with a distinguished medium vertex $\med$ adjacent to every
other vertex. Its single primitive is the \emph{cut}: a commitment that fixes a
set of edges as realised separations, deposits residue
$\res=\wt(\cut(S))\ge\flo$, and advances a monotone counter $M$. Every surface
verb---\shk{fold}, \shk{$\sim$}, \shk{close}, \shk{track}, \shk{catalyze},
\shk{mutate}, \shk{complete}---is the cut at some arity.

Composition is residue chaining: a chain $C=(S_1,\dots,S_k)$ commits total
boundary $\sum_i\res_i$ and $k$ cut events. This is the language's one
composition rule, and it is \emph{extensive}: it adds, it grows with the length
of the chain, and it is invariant under reordering. That last property is not
an accident but a theorem---the reversal lemma
\citep{sachikonye2026medium}---and the language draws a sharp negative
consequence from it: no predicate definable from the residues, the cut count,
or the committed boundary of a chain distinguishes a chain from its reversal.
The medium paper answers that limitation for \emph{orientation} by going
outside the chain, to the medium.

There is a second question the chain cannot answer, and it is not about
direction. Given a process that reaches its target, which step accomplished
most of the reaching? Total committed boundary does not say: a step may commit
a large boundary and move the process barely at all, and the sum records only
that the boundary was committed. Sensitivity, control, and the notion of a
rate-limiting step are all outside what an extensive aggregate can express.

\subsection{What this paper adds}

We add a second composition rule, and we are explicit that it is a second one,
because the specification claims there is exactly one
(\cref{rem:amend-composition}).

A \emph{rung} carries exactly one datum: its \emph{power} $\pow\in[0,1]$, the
fraction of the remaining above-floor gap that it closes. A \emph{ladder} is a
finite sequence of rungs, and its composite power is
\begin{equation}
\pow(\Rung_1\cdots\Rung_n) \;=\; 1-\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\pow_i).
\label{eq:compose-intro}
\end{equation}
This is \emph{intensive} where residue chaining is extensive: it is a fraction
rather than a total, it is bounded by $1$ however long the ladder, and adding a
rung of power $0$ changes nothing. The two rules coexist because they answer
different questions about the same chain---how much boundary was committed,
and how much of the distance was closed---and \cref{sec:twoRules} shows that
neither determines the other.

Three contributions are specific to this paper.

\paragraph{Power is derived, not declared.} The prior formulation took powers
as measured inputs and said so as a limitation. Here a rung's power is computed
from the \emph{separation cost} $\sepc(v)$---the minimum weight of a cut
placing $v$ on one side and the medium on the other---evaluated on a ball of
declared radius $\rad$ around $v$. The radius is a \emph{resolution parameter,
not a threshold on a score}: raising it admits more of the graph into the
computation, so a coarse ladder matches more permissively and a fine one
discriminates more. \Cref{sec:derived} gives the construction and
\cref{sec:results} the measured radius sweep.

\paragraph{Intensivity is a condition, and it is delicate.} If power depended
on what else was in the graph, composition would depend on the order in which
rungs were derived and \cref{eq:compose-intro} would be the wrong law. We
state intensivity as a requirement and test it. It fails for the obvious
construction: a local cut key normalised by the \emph{global} floor is not
intensive, because $\flo$ is a minimum over every edge and one distant small
edge moves it everywhere. Both the cut key and its normaliser must be local
(\cref{thm:intensive}). We report this rather than presenting only the
construction that works, because the near-miss is what localises the failure,
and because the test was built so that it could fail.

The honest form of the consequence is graded. Under sequential derivation---in
which each rung sees the graph its predecessors have modified---intensivity
reduces order-dependence by an order of magnitude and monotonically in
locality, but does not abolish it (\cref{prop:graded}). We state the graded
result rather than the clean one because the clean one is false.

\paragraph{The ladder refuses.} The language holds that a representation earns
a positive result only if some presentable object would have received a
negative one \citep{sachikonye2026medium}. A construct that always succeeds
classifies nothing. The ladder refuses a declared target its declared rungs
cannot reach, and the refusal is \emph{tight}: it rejects no ladder that could
have succeeded (\cref{thm:tight}). \Cref{sec:refusal} shows the refusal is
non-vacuous by exhibiting presentable ladders that trigger it.

\subsection{What we do not claim}

We do not claim the ladder is better than residue chaining; it answers a
different question, and \cref{sec:twoRules} is careful that neither aggregate
determines the other. We do not derive powers from chemical structure---the
derivation is from the contact graph, which is itself a model. We do not claim
exact order-independence, and \cref{prop:graded} says what we measured instead.
We do not claim the extension is conservative in the strong sense that no
existing program changes meaning; it is conservative in the weaker and checkable
sense that no existing rule is modified and the added rules apply only to added
syntax (\cref{prop:conservative}).

\subsection{The two claims this paper falsifies}

An extension that quietly contradicts its host is worse than one that does so
openly. Two published claims are falsified by what follows, and we amend both
in place rather than leaving a reader to find them.

\begin{remark}[Amendment: one composition rule becomes two]
\label{rem:amend-composition}
The specification states that Shakespeare has ``one verb (the cut) and one
composition rule (residue chaining)''
\citep{sachikonye2026shakespeare}. With the ladder this is false as written.
The verb claim survives---a rung commits a cut and nothing else---but the
composition claim must read: \emph{one verb, and two composition rules over its
chains, one extensive and one intensive}. \Cref{sec:twoRules} shows the two are
independent, so the amendment is not a restatement.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Amendment: the free observation is a class, not an instance]
\label{rem:amend-free}
The medium paper states of role observation that ``this is the one Shakespeare
observation that is free, and it is free because it individuates nothing new''
\citep{sachikonye2026medium}. The reason is correct and we adopt it verbatim;
the uniqueness is not. Reading a ladder's composite power is free for exactly
the same reason. The claim should read: \emph{an observation is free precisely
when it individuates nothing new, and role observation was the first such}.
\Cref{thm:free} makes the criterion a theorem rather than a case-by-case
licence, which is what turns the amendment into a strengthening.
\end{remark}

\section{Graph, Floor, Separation Cost}
\label{sec:substrate}

We restate what the extension builds on. Nothing in this section is new; it is
here so that the paper is self-contained and so that the exact form of each
quantity is fixed before it is used.

\begin{definition}[Contact graph]
\label{def:graph}
A \emph{contact graph} is a finite weighted graph $\Gph=(V,E,\wt)$ with a
distinguished \emph{medium} vertex $\med\in V$ adjacent to every other vertex,
and $\wt:E\to\Rpos$ strictly positive. Vertices $v\neq\med$ are \emph{items};
an edge between two items is a \emph{contact}; $\wt(e)$ is the cost of the
separation it maintains. We write $\Tot=\sum_{e\in E}\wt(e)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cut, residue, floor]
\label{def:cut}
For $S\subseteq V$ the \emph{cut} is
$\cut(S)=\{\,\{u,v\}\in E : \abs{\{u,v\}\cap S}=1\,\}$ with weight
$\wt(\cut(S))=\sum_{e\in\cut(S)}\wt(e)$. A set $S$ is \emph{admissible} if it
contains at least one item and omits $\med$. The \emph{floor} is
$\flo=\min_{e\in E}\wt(e)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Positive floor]
\label{lem:floor}
$\flo>0$, and $\wt(\cut(S))\ge\flo$ for every admissible $S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$E$ is finite and non-empty and every weight is strictly positive, so the
minimum is attained at a strictly positive value. An admissible $S$ contains
some item $v$ and omits $\med$, so $\{v,\med\}\in\cut(S)$ and the cut weight is
at least $\wt(\{v,\med\})\ge\flo$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finiteness does the work]
\label{rem:finiteness}
\Cref{lem:floor} uses finiteness twice and cannot be repaired without it. An
infinite edge set of strictly positive weights may have infimum zero: the
weights $1/n$ are each positive and admit no positive lower bound. The floor is
a property of a finite system, and every result below inherits that hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Separation cost]
\label{def:sepcost}
For an item $v$ the \emph{separation cost} is
\begin{equation}
\sepc(v)\;=\;\min_{S\ni v,\ \med\notin S}\wt(\cut(S)),
\label{eq:sepcost}
\end{equation}
the minimum weight of a cut placing $v$ on one side and the medium on the
other. The minimum is over a finite non-empty family, hence attained, and
equals a maximum flow \citep{fordfulkerson1956}.
\end{definition}

By \cref{lem:floor}, $\sepc(v)\ge\flo>0$ for every item.

\begin{remark}[Why the medium is the reference]
\label{rem:medium-ref}
\Cref{eq:sepcost} measures $v$ against the medium and not against another item.
This is forced: the medium is by construction what has no surroundings within
$\Gph$, so it is the only vertex against which a separation cost is well posed
without a further choice. Measuring $v$ against an arbitrary item $u$ would
make the quantity depend on $u$, and a power so defined would not be a property
of $v$.
\end{remark}

\section{Resolution: the Ball and the Local Floor}
\label{sec:resolution}

The separation cost of \cref{eq:sepcost} ranges over every subset of the graph.
We want a quantity that can be computed from a bounded neighbourhood, both
because that is what makes it intensive (\cref{sec:derived}) and because the
bound is itself a meaningful parameter.

\begin{definition}[Ball]
\label{def:ball}
The \emph{ball} of radius $\rad\in\N$ about an item $v$ is the set
$B_\rad(v)$ of items reachable from $v$ by at most $\rad$ contacts, where the
medium is not traversed. Thus $B_0(v)=\{v\}$, and $B_{\rad+1}(v)$ adds the item
neighbours of $B_\rad(v)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The medium is not traversed, and why]
\label{rem:no-traverse}
Excluding $\med$ from traversal is not a convenience. The medium is adjacent to
every item, so a path through it joins any two items in two steps and every
ball of radius $2$ would be the entire graph. The resolution parameter would
then have exactly two settings and would not be a resolution parameter. Items
are near each other when they share contacts, not when they share the medium.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Local separation cost and local floor]
\label{def:local}
The \emph{local separation cost} at radius $\rad$ is
\begin{equation}
\sepc_\rad(v)\;=\;\min_{v\in S\subseteq B_\rad(v)}\wt(\cut(S)),
\label{eq:local-sepcost}
\end{equation}
the minimum taken over subsets of the ball, with the cut still evaluated in the
whole graph. The \emph{local floor} is
$\flo_\rad(v)=\min\{\wt(e) : e\subseteq B_\rad(v)\cup\{\med\}\}$, the least
edge weight among edges internal to the ball together with its medium edges.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The local floor is a floor]
\label{lem:localfloor}
$0<\flo\le\flo_\rad(v)\le\sepc_\rad(v)$ for every item $v$ and radius $\rad$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\flo_\rad(v)$ is a minimum over a non-empty subfamily of $E$---non-empty
because $\{v,\med\}$ is always in it---so it is attained, is at least the
minimum over all of $E$, which is $\flo>0$, and the first two inequalities
follow. For the third, the minimising $S$ in \cref{eq:local-sepcost} contains
$v$ and omits $\med$, so $\{v,\med\}\in\cut(S)$ and
$\sepc_\rad(v)\ge\wt(\{v,\med\})\ge\flo_\rad(v)$, the last step because
$\{v,\med\}$ is one of the edges over which $\flo_\rad(v)$ minimises.
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Resolution, not threshold]
\label{prin:resolution}
The radius is a \emph{resolution parameter}, not a threshold on a score. It
does not set a level above which a quantity counts; it sets how much of the
graph enters the computation at all. Its effect is therefore monotone and
stateable in advance, and there is no setting at which it is uniformly
best---only settings appropriate to a question.
\end{principle}

\begin{proposition}[The direction of the sweep, and its mechanism]
\label{prop:sweep}
Raising the radius makes the derived power identify \emph{more} pairs of items,
not fewer. The mechanism is ball overlap: at radius $0$ the balls are disjoint
singletons and each item minimises over its own incident edges, so distinct
items generically receive distinct powers; as the radius grows the balls
overlap and eventually coincide, so distinct items minimise over nearly the
same edge set and their powers converge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Ball overlap is non-decreasing in the radius, since $B_\rad(v)\subseteq
B_{\rad+1}(v)$ for every $v$, and on a connected item graph of diameter $d$
every ball of radius at least $d$ is the whole item set, at which point
$\sepc_\rad$ and $\flo_\rad$ are the same function for every item and all
powers coincide. At radius $0$, $B_0(v)=\{v\}$ and the minimisation in
\cref{eq:local-sepcost} ranges over the single set $\{v\}$, giving
$\sepc_0(v)=\wt(\cut(\{v\}))$, the weighted degree, which separates items with
distinct neighbourhoods. The identification count is therefore non-decreasing
in the radius and strictly increasing wherever the overlap strictly increases.
\Cref{sec:results} reports both quantities.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[We predicted the opposite, and the sweep corrected us]
\label{rem:res-inverted}
Our stated expectation was that a coarse radius would identify more, by analogy
with a matcher in which a wider neighbourhood discriminates more finely. The
measurement is the reverse, and \cref{prop:sweep} says why: in that setting the
radius enlarges the \emph{description} of each item, whereas here it enlarges
the \emph{domain of a minimisation} that different items come to share.
Enlarging a description separates; enlarging a shared minimisation merges. We
record the correction, and we report the mean ball overlap beside the
identification count, because a count without its mechanism would leave the
direction unexplained and would invite the same mistake again.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What \cref{prin:resolution} rules out]
\label{rem:res-rules-out}
A threshold can be tuned to a desired outcome, and its value is a free
parameter a reader must take on trust. A resolution cannot be tuned in the same
way, because its effect is monotone and its direction is a stated consequence
rather than a fitted one. \Cref{sec:results} reports the whole sweep rather
than a chosen value, and a reader may read off the setting their question
requires: a fine radius to tell items apart, a coarse one to group them.
\end{remark}

\section{Derived Power}
\label{sec:derived}

\begin{definition}[Rung and power]
\label{def:rung}
A \emph{rung} carries exactly one datum, its \emph{power} $\pow\in[0,1]$. A
rung of power $0$ is \emph{inert}; a rung of power $1$ is \emph{absolute}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[A rung has no identity field]
\label{rem:noidentity}
\Cref{def:rung} is complete as stated: a rung is a number. It records nothing
about what performs the step, how long it takes, or what passes through it.
This is not brevity. A formalism in which rungs carried names would permit an
observable that read the names, and the claim that identities do not enter
would then be a claim about which observables one had chosen to write down
rather than about the formalism. The implementation enforces the same
discipline---the rung type has one field---so that a test of
label-independence is not testing the labels.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Derived power]
\label{def:derived}
Let $\Gph$ be a contact graph, $v$ an item and $\rad$ a radius. The
\emph{derived power} of $v$ at radius $\rad$ is
\begin{equation}
\pow_\rad(v)\;=\;1-\frac{\flo_\rad(v)}{\sepc_\rad(v)}.
\label{eq:derived}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Derived power is a power]
\label{lem:derived-bounded}
$\pow_\rad(v)\in[0,1)$ for every item and radius.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:localfloor}, $0<\flo_\rad(v)\le\sepc_\rad(v)$, so the ratio lies
in $(0,1]$ and $\pow_\rad(v)\in[0,1)$. The value $1$ is not attained, which is
the floor asserting itself: no single rung closes the whole gap, because
closing it entirely would require separating $v$ at zero cost.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading \cref{eq:derived}]
\label{rem:reading-derived}
The quantity compares what it costs to separate $v$ from the medium against the
cheapest separation available anywhere in its neighbourhood. An item that is
expensive to separate relative to its surroundings has high power: committing
it closes much of the remaining distance. An item whose separation cost is
already at the local floor has power near zero: there is nothing left to close.
The floor appears in the numerator and not as a subtracted constant because
what a rung closes is a \emph{fraction} of the above-floor gap, and the floor
is the part of the gap that cannot be closed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Intensivity}

\begin{definition}[Intensive and extensive]
\label{def:intext}
Let $f$ assign a real number to an item of a contact graph. Call $f$
\emph{intensive at radius $\rad$} if $f(v)$ is unchanged by any modification of
the graph outside $B_\rad(v)$ that leaves the edges of $B_\rad(v)$ and their
medium edges intact, and \emph{extensive} otherwise.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Derived power is intensive]
\label{thm:intensive}
$\pow_\rad$ is intensive at radius $\rad$. Moreover both factors are required:
the quantity $1-\flo/\sepc_\rad(v)$, in which the cut key is local but the
normaliser is the global floor, is \emph{not} intensive at any radius.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the first claim, $\sepc_\rad(v)$ minimises over subsets of $B_\rad(v)$, and
for such an $S$ the cut $\cut(S)$ consists of edges with exactly one endpoint
in $S\subseteq B_\rad(v)$. Every such edge is either internal to $B_\rad(v)$,
or joins $B_\rad(v)$ to the medium, or joins $B_\rad(v)$ to an item outside it;
in the last case the outside endpoint is a neighbour of a ball member, hence
lies in $B_{\rad+1}(v)$, and the edge is determined by the ball's own incident
edge set. A modification outside $B_\rad(v)$ preserving those edges therefore
preserves every term of the minimisation, hence preserves $\sepc_\rad(v)$. The
same argument applies to $\flo_\rad(v)$, which minimises over a subset of the
same edges. The ratio is therefore unchanged.

For the second claim it suffices to exhibit a modification that changes $\flo$
without touching $B_\rad(v)$. Adjoin to $\Gph$ a fresh item $u$ outside
$B_\rad(v)$, joined to the medium by an edge of weight $\flo/2$. The new graph
is a contact graph, $B_\rad(v)$ is untouched, so $\sepc_\rad(v)$ is unchanged;
but the global floor is now $\flo/2$, so $1-\flo/\sepc_\rad(v)$ strictly
increases. Hence the quantity is not intensive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[We got this wrong first, and the near-miss is the informative
control]
\label{rem:nearmiss}
The construction we tried first was $1-\flo/\sepc_\rad(v)$: a local cut key
normalised by the ambient floor, which is the floor the language already
declares and therefore the one nearest to hand. It fails, and
\cref{thm:intensive} says why. We keep it as a control rather than deleting it,
for a reason worth stating. A control that differs from the candidate in every
respect shows only that \emph{some} difference matters. This one differs in a
single component and therefore localises the failure to the normaliser. In the
measurements of \cref{sec:results} the three quantities drift by exactly $0$,
by $1.6\times10^{-2}$ and by $9.4\times10^{-2}$ respectively, and the ordering
of the middle term is the whole content of the diagnosis.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The floor is load-bearing only through positivity]
\label{cor:positivity-only}
Every result in this section uses $\flo_\rad(v)$ only through the facts that it
is strictly positive and is attained on a finite set. Any other rule assigning
a strictly positive local normaliser to each ball yields the same theorems with
different constants. In particular no result determines the value $\flo$, and
none is disturbed by rescaling every weight by a positive constant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:localfloor} and \cref{lem:derived-bounded} use only positivity and
attainment. \Cref{thm:intensive} uses only that the normaliser is computed from
the ball. Under $\wt\mapsto c\wt$ with $c>0$ both $\flo_\rad$ and $\sepc_\rad$
scale by $c$ and \cref{eq:derived} is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\section{Composition}
\label{sec:composition}

\begin{definition}[Ladder]
\label{def:ladder}
A \emph{ladder} is a finite sequence of rungs $\Rung_1,\dots,\Rung_n$ with
power sequence $(\pow_1,\dots,\pow_n)$, applied in order, each to the state its
predecessor produced.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicative composition]
\label{thm:multiplicative}
Write $\gap_i$ for the above-floor gap remaining after $i$ rungs, with
$\gap_0>0$ the initial gap. If each rung closes the fraction $\pow_i$ of the
gap it is presented with, then $\gap_i=(1-\pow_i)\gap_{i-1}$, hence
\begin{equation}
\gap_n=\gap_0\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\pow_i),
\qquad
\pow(\Lad)=1-\frac{\gap_n}{\gap_0}=1-\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\pow_i).
\label{eq:compose}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence is \cref{def:rung} rearranged. Induction on $n$ gives the
product. The composite power is the fraction of the initial gap closed, which
is $1-\gap_n/\gap_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded, monotone, and never complete]
\label{cor:bounded}
$\pow(\Lad)\in[0,1)$ whenever every $\pow_i<1$; it is non-decreasing under
appending a rung; and appending a rung of power $0$ leaves it unchanged.
Repeating a rung of power $\pow\in(0,1)$ $n$ times gives $1-(1-\pow)^n$, which
tends to $1$ and equals it at no finite $n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each factor $(1-\pow_i)\in(0,1]$, so the product lies in $(0,1]$ and the
composite in $[0,1)$. Appending multiplies the product by a factor at most $1$,
so the composite does not decrease; a factor of exactly $1$ leaves it fixed.
The repetition formula is \cref{eq:compose} with all powers equal, and
$(1-\pow)^n>0$ for all finite $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sensitivity is transmission]
\label{prop:sensitivity}
$\dfrac{\partial\pow(\Lad)}{\partial\pow_j}=\prod_{i\neq j}(1-\pow_i)
 =\dfrac{P}{1-\pow_j}$, where $P=\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$ is the residual fraction.
Within a ladder this is strictly increasing in $\pow_j$: control lies at the
\emph{highest}-power rung.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \cref{eq:compose}: only the $j$-th factor depends on $\pow_j$ and
it does so with derivative $-1$, so the derivative of the product is
$-\prod_{i\neq j}(1-\pow_i)$ and the composite's derivative is its negation.
Dividing and multiplying by $(1-\pow_j)$ gives the second form, which is
increasing in $\pow_j$ at fixed $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The direction is the opposite of the naive expectation]
\label{rem:direction}
The expectation is that the weakest rung is the bottleneck and therefore the
place where improvement pays. \Cref{prop:sensitivity} says the opposite, and
the reason is that the quantity being maximised is \emph{transmission} rather
than slack: a marginal gain at rung $j$ reaches the output only through the
remaining rungs, and the product $\prod_{i\neq j}(1-\pow_i)$ is what survives
that passage. Improving a weak rung buys a large local change that the rest of
the ladder then discards. This is a prediction and it is sharp enough to fail;
\cref{sec:results} reports it measured.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Order dependence, stated as measured}
\label{sec:order}

\Cref{eq:compose} is symmetric in its arguments, so permuting a \emph{fixed}
power sequence cannot change the composite. That fact is a property of the
formula and carries no information about the derivation, and a test built on it
cannot fail. We say so explicitly because such a test is easy to write and
easy to mistake for evidence.

\begin{remark}[A test that cannot fail is not evidence]
\label{rem:nondisc}
Permuting a fixed multiset of powers and observing that the composite is
unchanged verifies the commutativity of multiplication. Every candidate
definition of power passes it, including deliberately extensive ones. Our suite
runs this comparison and reports it as \textsc{non-discriminating}, excluded
from the score, with this reason recorded (\cref{sec:results}). It is retained
because a reader cannot distinguish a passing control from a vacuous one by
inspecting a table of successes.
\end{remark}

The test that can fail derives the powers \emph{sequentially}: each rung's power
is computed on the graph as its predecessors left it, so a different order
presents each rung with a different graph.

\begin{definition}[Sequential derivation]
\label{def:seqderiv}
Given an ordering $v_1,\dots,v_n$ of items, the \emph{sequentially derived}
power sequence is obtained by setting $\pow_i=\pow_\rad(v_i)$ evaluated in
$\Gph_{i-1}$, where $\Gph_0=\Gph$ and $\Gph_i$ is $\Gph_{i-1}$ with the
weakest item--item contact incident to $v_i$ removed.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Order dependence is graded, not abolished]
\label{prop:graded}
Under sequential derivation the composite power is in general
order-dependent, for intensive and extensive candidates alike. Intensivity
reduces the dependence but does not remove it, and the reduction is monotone in
locality.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof and measurement]
That some dependence survives is immediate: \cref{def:seqderiv} modifies the
graph between derivations, and \cref{thm:intensive} guarantees invariance only
against modifications \emph{outside} the ball. A removal incident to $v_i$ lies
inside the balls of $v_i$'s neighbours, so it is exactly the case the theorem
does not cover.

The size of the residual dependence is a measurement, not a theorem. Over all
$720$ orderings of a six-item chain, the number of distinct composite values
was $47$ for the intensive candidate, $118$ for the near-miss and $421$ for the
extensive control, with spreads $1.07\times10^{-2}$, $1.09\times10^{-2}$ and
$1.04\times10^{-1}$. At radius $0$ the intensive count falls to $11$. The
ordering of these numbers is the content of the proposition; their values are
properties of the instance and we do not generalise them.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why we state the graded result]
\label{rem:graded-honest}
We set out to prove that intensivity buys exact order-independence. It does
not, and the measurement above is what showed it. Reporting the clean claim
would have required either restricting to ladders whose balls never overlap---a
condition satisfied by few interesting processes---or declining to mutate the
graph, which would have made the test unable to fail. The graded statement is
weaker and true, and it comes with a mechanism: dependence enters exactly where
commitment reaches inside a neighbouring ball.
\end{remark}

\section{Two Composition Rules, Neither Determining the Other}
\label{sec:twoRules}

Residue chaining and ladder composition are both defined on a chain of cuts.
This section shows they are independent, which is what justifies keeping both
and what makes \cref{rem:amend-composition} an amendment rather than a
restatement.

\begin{definition}[The two aggregates]
\label{def:aggregates}
For a chain $C=(S_1,\dots,S_n)$ with residues $\res_i=\wt(\cut(S_i))$ and
powers $\pow_i$, the \emph{extensive aggregate} is $R(C)=\sum_i\res_i$ and the
\emph{intensive aggregate} is $\pow(C)=1-\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Independence]
\label{prop:independent}
Neither aggregate determines the other. There exist chains with equal $R$ and
different $\pow$, and chains with equal $\pow$ and different $R$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first, take two chains of two cuts each, with residues $(2\flo,2\flo)$
in both cases, so $R=4\flo$ for each. Let the first close gap fractions
$(0.5,0.5)$ and the second $(0.9,0.1)$; the composites are $0.75$ and $0.91$.
Residue is the boundary committed and power is the distance closed, and nothing
ties them: a cut may commit much boundary while closing little gap.

For the second, take chains with powers $(0.5,0.5)$ and $(0.75)$, both of
composite $0.75$, whose residues are $2\flo$ and $\flo$ respectively; the
extensive aggregates differ because the chains have different lengths.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The reversal lemma does not transfer]
\label{cor:reversal-transfer}
The reversal lemma establishes that $R$ is invariant under reversing a chain.
The same holds for $\pow$ by symmetry of \cref{eq:compose}. Consequently the
ladder inherits the limitation the medium paper draws from that lemma: no
predicate definable from a ladder's powers alone orients a process, and
orientation must still be sought in the medium.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Symmetry of the product under permutation gives the invariance. The negative
consequence is then the same argument as for $R$: a quantity invariant under
reversal cannot distinguish a chain from its reversal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[We record the limitation rather than leaving it to be found]
\label{rem:limitation-inherited}
\Cref{cor:reversal-transfer} is a limitation of the construct introduced in
this paper, and a reader who noticed the symmetry of \cref{eq:compose} would
reach it unaided. The ladder answers ``how much of the distance did this step
close'' and does not answer ``which way does the process run''; the second
question was answered elsewhere and the ladder does not reopen it.
\end{remark}

\section{Syntax}
\label{sec:syntax}

We add four productions. The remainder of the grammar is unchanged, and we
reproduce only what the additions touch.

\begin{definition}[Added grammar]
\label{def:grammar}
\begin{lstlisting}
ladderExpr   ::= ladder [ rungList ] [ at radius number ]
rungList     ::= rungItem { , rungItem }
rungItem     ::= power number | rung ident | derived ident
climbExpr    ::= climb expr with ladderExpr [ reach number ]
observeLad   ::= observe expr as power
\end{lstlisting}
The keyword set gains \shk{ladder}, \shk{rung}, \shk{power}, \shk{derived},
\shk{radius}, \shk{climb} and \shk{reach}. The nonterminal \textit{expr} and
all existing productions are untouched.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Filling a gap rather than widening one]
\label{rem:gap}
The host specification declares a \shk{medium} block by example and gives it no
production, and its type grammar omits \textsf{Role} although a rule concludes
at that type. We do not repair those omissions here---they belong to the papers
that introduced them---but we avoid adding to them: every construct below has a
production in \cref{def:grammar}, a type in \cref{def:types}, a typing rule in
\cref{sec:typing} and a reduction rule in \cref{sec:opsem}.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[A ladder over a folded receiver]
\label{ex:program}
\begin{lstlisting}
receiver bio
floor 3.7e-4

medium cytosol {
  ambient  "H2O" = 55.5
  exchange 1.0e-3
}

P := fold "1CRN"

-- powers declared explicitly
L1 := ladder [ power 0.45, power 0.30, power 0.55, power 0.20 ]

-- powers derived from the receiver at a declared resolution
L2 := ladder [ derived P ] at radius 1

observe L1 as power            -- free: reads committed boundary
climb P with L1 reach 0.80     -- commits; M advances by four
\end{lstlisting}
The first \shk{observe} is free. The \shk{climb} is not: it performs four
commitments and advances the clock by four.
\end{example}

\section{Types}
\label{sec:typing}

\begin{definition}[Added types]
\label{def:types}
The type grammar gains two constructors:
\[
\tau ::= \cdots \mid \mathsf{Rung} \mid \mathsf{Ladder}.
\]
A value of type $\mathsf{Rung}$ carries a power in $[0,1]$; a value of type
$\mathsf{Ladder}$ carries a finite sequence of such. Both are values in the
sense of the host judgement and carry a floor annotation.
\end{definition}

The judgement is the host's, $\Gph\vdash e:\tau\,@\,\flo$, read: in context
$\Gph$ fixing the ambient floor and receiver, $e$ has type $\tau$ and is
individuated at floor $\flo>0$.

\begin{rulename}[Rung introduction]
\label{rule:rung}
\[
\frac{\Gph\vdash p:\mathsf{Scalar}\,@\,\flo \qquad 0\le p\le 1}
     {\Gph\vdash \shk{power}\ p:\mathsf{Rung}\,@\,\flo}
\]
A power outside $[0,1]$ is ill-typed. The bound is not a runtime check: by
\cref{lem:derived-bounded} a derived power satisfies it by construction, and a
declared one is checked where it is written.
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[Derived rung]
\label{rule:derived}
\[
\frac{\Gph\vdash v:\mathsf{Leaf}_\kappa\,@\,\flo \qquad
      \rad\in\N \qquad \med \text{ declared}}
     {\Gph\vdash \shk{derived}\ v\ \shk{at radius}\ \rad
      :\mathsf{Rung}\,@\,\flo}
\]
The premise $\med$ \emph{declared} is the same side condition the host requires
for role observation, and for the same reason: \cref{eq:sepcost} measures
against the medium, so without a medium the power is undefined rather than
zero.
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[Ladder formation]
\label{rule:ladder}
\[
\frac{\Gph\vdash \Rung_i:\mathsf{Rung}\,@\,\flo \quad (1\le i\le n)}
     {\Gph\vdash \shk{ladder}\ [\Rung_1,\dots,\Rung_n]
      :\mathsf{Ladder}\,@\,\flo}
\]
A ladder is well-typed at the floor its rungs share. Rungs derived at different
radii may be combined; the radius is a property of a derivation, not of a rung.
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[Power observation]
\label{rule:obspower}
\[
\frac{\Gph\vdash \Lad:\mathsf{Ladder}\,@\,\flo}
     {\Gph\vdash \shk{observe}\ \Lad\ \shk{as}\ \shk{power}
      :\mathsf{Scalar}\,@\,\flo}
\]
Observing a composite power commits no new cut: the computation reads powers
already carried by rungs already formed, so the clock $M$ does not advance.
This is free for exactly the reason role observation is free---it individuates
nothing new---and \cref{thm:free} states that reason as a criterion.
\end{rulename}

\section{Operational Semantics}
\label{sec:opsem}

Configurations are those of the host: $\langle\Gph_G,M,e\rangle$, the contact
graph, the committed-cut count, and the expression under evaluation.

\begin{rulename}[E-Power: forming a rung is free]
\label{rule:e-power}
\[
\frac{0\le p\le 1}
     {\langle\Gph_G,M,\shk{power}\ p\rangle \to
      \langle\Gph_G,\,M,\,v_{\mathsf{Rung}}(p)\rangle}
\]
No boundary is committed and $M$ does not advance: declaring a number
individuates nothing.
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[E-Derive: deriving a rung is free]
\label{rule:e-derive}
\[
\frac{\pow_\rad(v)=1-\flo_\rad(v)/\sepc_\rad(v)}
     {\langle\Gph_G,M,\shk{derived}\ v\ \shk{at radius}\ \rad\rangle \to
      \langle\Gph_G,\,M,\,v_{\mathsf{Rung}}(\pow_\rad(v))\rangle}
\]
The derivation reads edge weights already present in $\Gph_G$ and computes a
minimum over them. It commits no cut, so $M$ does not advance. The graph is
read and not written.
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[E-Observe-Power: reading a composite is free]
\label{rule:e-obspower}
\[
\frac{\Lad=[\Rung_1,\dots,\Rung_n]}
     {\langle\Gph_G,M,\shk{observe}\ \Lad\ \shk{as}\ \shk{power}\rangle \to
      \langle\Gph_G,\,M,\,v_{\mathsf{Scalar}}
        (1-\textstyle\prod_i(1-\pow_i))\rangle}
\]
\end{rulename}

\begin{rulename}[E-Climb: climbing commits, once per rung]
\label{rule:e-climb}
\[
\frac{\begin{array}{c}
 \Lad=[\Rung_1,\dots,\Rung_n] \qquad
 S_i \text{ admissible} \qquad \wt(\cut(S_i))\ge\flo\\
 \gap_n=\gap_0\prod_i(1-\pow_i)
\end{array}}
     {\langle\Gph_G,M,\shk{climb}\ x\ \shk{with}\ \Lad\rangle \to
      \langle\Gph_G\oplus\textstyle\bigcup_i\cut(S_i),\,M+n,\,
       v_{\mathsf{Result}}(\gap_n)\rangle}
\]
Climbing performs one commitment per rung: the clock advances by $n$, and each
commitment deposits residue at least the floor. This is the sole rule of the
extension that is not free.
\end{rulename}

\begin{theorem}[A criterion for freeness]
\label{thm:free}
A reduction rule may leave $M$ unchanged if and only if it commits no cut,
that is, if it neither adds edges to the committed boundary nor requires a
boundary not already committed. Under this criterion \cref{rule:e-power},
\cref{rule:e-derive} and \cref{rule:e-obspower} are free and
\cref{rule:e-climb} is not, and the role observation of the host is free.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) If a rule commits no cut then the committed boundary and the cut
count are both unchanged, by the definition of the counter as a count of cut
events. Leaving $M$ fixed is therefore consistent with clock monotonicity,
which forbids only \emph{decrease} and requires increase only \emph{at a cut
event}.

($\Rightarrow$) If a rule committed a cut and left $M$ unchanged, two
configurations with the same graph and the same counter would differ in the
boundary committed, contradicting that the counter records cut events.

The classification is then immediate. \Cref{rule:e-power} constructs a value
from a literal; \cref{rule:e-derive} computes minima over existing weights;
\cref{rule:e-obspower} multiplies numbers carried by values already formed.
None writes to $\Gph_G$. \Cref{rule:e-climb} writes
$\Gph_G\oplus\bigcup_i\cut(S_i)$ and advances by $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The criterion is the argument of the host, generalised]
\label{rem:free-general}
The host licenses one free observation with a one-sentence reason: the
comparison reads boundary that is already committed, so it individuates nothing
new. \Cref{thm:free} does not weaken that reason, it promotes it. What was a
licence granted to one rule becomes a criterion that any rule may be tested
against. This is the sense in which \cref{rem:amend-free} is a strengthening
rather than a concession: a uniqueness claim is replaced by a decidable test,
and role observation passes it.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Progress]
\label{thm:progress}
If $\Gph\vdash e:\tau\,@\,\flo$ and $e$ is not a value, some reduction applies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By cases on the added forms. \shk{power}~$p$ is well-typed only with
$0\le p\le1$, which is the premise of \cref{rule:e-power}. \shk{derived}~$v$ is
well-typed only with $\med$ declared, and then $\sepc_\rad(v)$ is a minimum
over a finite non-empty family, hence defined, and by \cref{lem:localfloor}
strictly positive, so \cref{rule:e-derive} applies. A ladder of well-typed
rungs is a value. The form \shk{observe}~$\Lad$~\shk{as}~\shk{power} applies
\cref{rule:e-obspower} unconditionally. For \shk{climb}, admissibility of each
$S_i$ follows from the typing of the rungs, and the residue side condition
holds by \cref{lem:floor}, so \cref{rule:e-climb} applies. Existing forms are
covered by the cases of the host, which the extension does not modify.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Preservation]
\label{thm:preservation}
If $\Gph\vdash e:\tau\,@\,\flo$ and $\langle\Gph_G,M,e\rangle\to
\langle\Gph_G',M',v\rangle$, then $v$ has type $\tau$ and floor $\flo$, with
$\flo$ unchanged and $M'\ge M$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each added rule produces a value of the type its typing rule assigns:
\cref{rule:e-power} and \cref{rule:e-derive} produce $\mathsf{Rung}$,
\cref{rule:e-obspower} a $\mathsf{Scalar}$, \cref{rule:e-climb} a
$\mathsf{Result}$. The floor is a component of the context and no added rule
modifies it. Three rules leave $M$ fixed and one increments it by $n\ge0$, so
$M'\ge M$. \Cref{rule:e-climb} mutates $\Gph_G$; the mutation retains the
medium and every weight remains strictly positive, so \cref{lem:floor}
continues to apply to the mutated graph and the floor annotation remains valid.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Soundness]
\label{cor:soundness}
A well-typed program in the extended language does not get stuck, and every
commitment it performs deposits residue at least $\flo>0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Clock monotonicity is preserved]
\label{thm:monotone}
$M$ is non-decreasing along every evaluation of the extended language, and
increases strictly at every cut event. Re-evaluating a \shk{climb} is a new
sequence of commitments at strictly higher $M$, never a cached retrieval;
re-evaluating a free form is not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
No added rule decrements $M$. \Cref{rule:e-climb} increments by the number of
cut events it performs, and the three free rules perform none, so by
\cref{thm:free} their leaving $M$ fixed is consistent with the requirement that
increase occur at every cut event. A cache returning a previous \shk{climb}
result without incrementing would contradict \cref{rule:e-climb}, which
increments unconditionally.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A repair to a proof of the host, not to its theorem]
\label{rem:monotone-repair}
The proof of clock monotonicity in the host asserts in passing that every
value-producing rule increments $M$. That parenthetical is already false in the
host, since role observation produces a value of type $\mathsf{Role}$ without
incrementing, and it is false three more times here. The theorem itself is
unaffected: what it requires is that no rule decrements and that every
\emph{cut event} increments. We state the repaired form, and observe that
\cref{thm:free} supplies exactly the side condition the original proof left
implicit.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The extension is conservative on existing programs]
\label{prop:conservative}
Let $p$ be a program containing none of the added keywords. Then $p$ has the
same typing derivations and the same reductions in the extended language as in
the host, and yields the same value at the same final $M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The added typing rules have conclusions whose subjects are \shk{power},
\shk{derived}, \shk{ladder}, \shk{climb}, or \shk{observe} $\cdots$
\shk{as power}; the added reduction rules likewise. None of these forms occurs
in $p$, so no added rule is applicable at any point in a derivation or
reduction of $p$. No existing rule is modified. Hence the derivations and
reduction sequences are literally the same objects, and the final value and
counter agree.
\end{proof}

\section{Static Analysis and the Second Refusal}
\label{sec:refusal}

\subsection{Deciding reachability before execution}

\begin{proposition}[Reachability is decidable statically]
\label{prop:static}
Whether a ladder with declared powers $(\pow_1,\dots,\pow_n)$ attains a
declared target $\pow^\star$ is decidable in $O(n)$ arithmetic operations
without evaluating any \shk{climb}, and therefore without committing any cut or
advancing $M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compute $\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$ in $n$ multiplications and compare $1$ minus it
against $\pow^\star$. By \cref{thm:multiplicative} this is exactly the value a
\shk{climb} would reach. The computation reads declared numbers and touches no
graph.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cost of a target]
\label{cor:cost}
Reaching $\pow^\star<1$ with copies of a rung of power $\pow\in(0,1)$ requires
exactly $n=\lceil \log(1-\pow^\star)/\log(1-\pow)\rceil$ rungs, and no finite
number suffices for $\pow^\star=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Solve $1-(1-\pow)^n\ge\pow^\star$ for $n$; both logarithms are negative so the
inequality direction is preserved on dividing. Minimality is the ceiling.
For $\pow^\star=1$ we would need $(1-\pow)^n\le0$, impossible for finite $n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The refusal}

The host holds that a representation earns a positive result only if some
presentable object would have received a negative one, and that a framework
with no refusals has an empty defined class. The medium paper discharged this
with two refusals, to individuate and to orient. The ladder supplies a third,
which is the second refusal of a \emph{construct} rather than of an
observation.

\begin{definition}[Refusal to climb]
\label{def:refuse-climb}
A ladder $\Lad$ with declared target $\pow^\star$ for which
$1-\prod_i(1-\pow_i)<\pow^\star$ is \emph{refused}: the program does not
evaluate, the verdict is $\textsf{subfloor}$ naming the shortfall
$\pow^\star-\pow(\Lad)$, and no cut is committed. A ladder with no rungs is
refused with verdict $\textsf{empty}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The verdict name is taken, not coined]
\label{rem:subfloor}
$\textsf{subfloor}$ is an existing verdict label in the reference
implementation of the surrounding framework, where it already means that a
quantity fell below a declared floor. We reuse it rather than introduce a
synonym. Similarly we do not reuse the label $\textsf{inert}$, which is bound
in that implementation to a different meaning, and we say
\emph{label-independent} where an unqualified reading of ``inert'' might
collide.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The refusal is tight]
\label{thm:tight}
No ladder refused by \cref{def:refuse-climb} could have reached its declared
target. The refusal therefore excludes no program that would have succeeded,
and every refused program would have failed at run time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\Lad$ is refused, so $1-\prod_i(1-\pow_i)<\pow^\star$. By
\cref{thm:multiplicative} the gap after all $n$ rungs is
$\gap_0\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$, so the fraction of the initial gap closed by
executing $\Lad$ in full is exactly $1-\prod_i(1-\pow_i)$, which is below
$\pow^\star$ by hypothesis. Executing the ladder therefore terminates short of
the target. Conversely a ladder not refused has composite at least
$\pow^\star$ and reaches it. The static verdict and the executed outcome
therefore agree on every ladder, which is the content of tightness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The refusal is non-vacuous]
\label{thm:nonvacuous}
Both refusals of \cref{def:refuse-climb} are attainable by ladders the language
can construct.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=2pt]
\item The ladder $\shk{ladder}\,[\,\shk{power}\ 0.1,\ \shk{power}\ 0.1\,]$ with
target $0.9$ has composite $0.19<0.9$ and is refused with
$\textsf{subfloor}$.
\item The ladder $\shk{ladder}\,[\;]$ is refused with $\textsf{empty}$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover refusal is not universal: the ladder
$\shk{ladder}\,[\,\shk{power}\ 0.5,\ \shk{power}\ 0.6\,]$ with target $0.7$ has
composite $0.8\ge0.7$ and is accepted. Hence the predicate separates
presentable ladders in both directions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation of \cref{eq:compose} in each case: $1-(0.9)(0.9)=0.19$;
the empty product is $1$ so the composite is $0$; and
$1-(0.5)(0.4)=0.8$. Each ladder is well-typed by \cref{rule:rung} and
\cref{rule:ladder}, hence presentable.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the third clause is part of the theorem]
\label{rem:both-directions}
A refusal that fired on every input would classify nothing, exactly as a
refusal that never fired classifies nothing. \Cref{thm:nonvacuous} therefore
exhibits an accepted ladder alongside the refused ones. The corresponding
requirement on the validation suite is that both verdicts occur in quantity,
and \cref{sec:results} reports the counts rather than only the agreement rate.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Saturation diagnostic]
\label{prop:saturation}
If every rung of a ladder of length $n$ has power at most $\pow_{\max}$, and
$1-(1-\pow_{\max})^n<\pow^\star$, then the ladder is refused whatever its
actual powers. The diagnostic is sound and is computed from $n$ and
$\pow_{\max}$ alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The composite is monotone non-decreasing in each power by
\cref{cor:bounded}, so it is maximised at $\pow_i=\pow_{\max}$ for all $i$,
giving $1-(1-\pow_{\max})^n$. If even that is below the target, no assignment
of powers within the bound reaches it.
\end{proof}

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The suite is three modules and a runner, at
\texttt{validation/} beside this paper. Every number quoted below is read from
the JSON the runner writes; none is entered by hand.

\paragraph{Scoring policy.} A check contributes to the score only if it could
have failed. A check whose own construction shows the statistic cannot
discriminate is marked \textsc{non-discriminating}, excluded from the score,
and its reason recorded. A claim the suite cannot bear on is marked
\textsc{not tested} and excluded. Both categories are reported rather than
omitted, because a reader cannot distinguish a passing control from a vacuous
one by inspecting a table of successes.

\paragraph{Negative controls.} Four checks are named with the prefix
\textsc{negative control} so that they can be harvested by name, following the
convention of the host suite. Each pairs with a positive claim and would
falsify it if it behaved differently.

\paragraph{Totals.} Twenty-three checks run. Twenty-one are scored and all
twenty-one pass; one is non-discriminating; one is not tested. Four of the
scored checks are negative controls.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The substrate}

The floor is computed rather than assumed on four instances, including an
adversarial one whose smallest weight is $10^{-12}$; every instance returns a
strictly positive floor. The failure branch is exhibited separately: the
sequence $1/n$ clears every $\varepsilon$ down to $10^{-9}$, so its infimum is
zero, and truncating the same sequence to ten stages restores a floor at
$0.1$. This is the content of \cref{rem:finiteness} made visible rather than
left tacit.

The local separation cost of \cref{eq:local-sepcost} is checked against
exhaustive enumeration of every bipartition on forty graphs: at a radius
covering the graph the two agree to within $10^{-12}$, which is a check of the
implementation against ground truth rather than a restatement of the
definition.

localises the failure: the cut key was already local, and locality of the cut

Sensitivity is checked two ways over $3000$ ladders: the closed form

returning most for a fixed improvement on every ladder. The second check is
independent of the first, so the counter-intuitive direction of
\cref{rem:direction} is established without assuming the formula that predicts
it.

Freeness and the clock behave as \cref{thm:free} requires: five thousand free
operations leave $M$ at zero, a climb of three rungs advances it to exactly
three and records three residues each at least the floor, and five hundred
identical commitments increment $M$ by exactly one apiece with no caching. The
paired negative control confirms that a commitment does register, so the first
result is about freeness and not about a counter that never moves.

\subsection{The refusal}

Over $5000$ generated programs the static verdict and the executed outcome
agree in every case, with $4032$ accepted and $968$ rejected. Both verdicts
occur in quantity, so the test could have failed in either direction; a suite
in which every program was accepted would not have tested rejection. This is
\cref{thm:tight} measured.

Three negative controls attend the refusal. It fires on a presentable ladder
that cannot reach its target and on an empty ladder, and declines to fire on a
ladder that can---so the predicate separates in both directions. A refused
ladder commits nothing: no residue is recorded and the graph is unchanged,
which is the operational content of a refusal that declines to individuate.
And the verdict type rejects a payload that does not match its label, so a
refusal cannot smuggle out a result.

\subsection{What the suite does not establish}

One check is recorded \textsc{not tested}: whether a derived power corresponds
to any rate measured in a laboratory. The powers here are derived from a
contact graph, which is itself a model, and no check in this suite bears on the
empirical question. We record it rather than leave the omission to be noticed.

One check is recorded \textsc{non-discriminating}: permuting a fixed multiset
of powers. Every candidate returns exactly one distinct composite, because the
composition law is symmetric in its arguments. The check cannot fail and is
excluded from the score, with that reason stored in the results file.

\section{Falsifiable Predictions}
\label{sec:predictions}

Each prediction is stated with the observation that would refute it.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.},leftmargin=3em,itemsep=0.6em]

\item \textbf{Multiplicative composition.} Composite power follows
\cref{eq:compose} and not an additive, maximum or averaging law.
\emph{Failure mode:} measured composite tracking $\sum_i\pow_i$ or
$\max_i\pow_i$ across synthesised ladders.

\item \textbf{Intensivity requires a local normaliser.} A power built from a
local cut key and the global floor is not intensive.
\emph{Failure mode:} the near-miss candidate showing zero drift under
extension, which would mean the global floor is not doing the damage we
attribute to it.

\item \textbf{Order dependence is graded.} Under sequential derivation the
intensive candidate admits strictly fewer distinct composites than the
extensive one, and strictly more than one.
\emph{Failure mode:} either exactly one---which would make the graded claim
too weak---or no separation between candidates, which would make it empty.

\item \textbf{Radius is a resolution.} Identification is monotone in the
radius and tracks ball overlap.
\emph{Failure mode:} a flat sweep, showing the parameter is inert; or
identification moving opposite to overlap, showing the mechanism is not the one
we give.

\item \textbf{Control at the strongest rung.} Sensitivity is
$P/(1-\pow_j)$, maximised at the highest-power rung.
\emph{Failure mode:} a finite-difference experiment ranking the weakest rung
first. This prediction is deliberately counter-intuitive and is the sharpest
in the set.

\item \textbf{Freeness.} A rule leaves $M$ unchanged exactly when it commits
no cut.
\emph{Failure mode:} a rule that commits a cut while leaving $M$ fixed, or a
free rule that advances it.

\item \textbf{Tightness.} The static refusal agrees with execution on every
ladder, and both verdicts occur.
\emph{Failure mode:} any disagreement, or a suite in which nothing is refused.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to Existing Accounts}
\label{sec:relation}

\paragraph{Metabolic control analysis.} Control coefficients summing to unity
are the closest existing account of where control sits in a chain. The
sensitivity of \cref{prop:sensitivity} is not a control coefficient: it is a
derivative of a composite \emph{fraction} rather than of a flux, and it is not
normalised to sum to one. The two therefore need not agree, and
\cref{rem:direction} is a prediction that can be tested against a measured
control distribution rather than a restatement of one.

\paragraph{Rate-limiting steps.} The notion that one step limits a process is
recovered here as the rung of highest power, and \cref{prop:sensitivity} says
that this is also where improvement pays. This inverts the usual advice to
improve the slowest step, and the inversion is a consequence of measuring
fractions of a gap rather than rates.

\paragraph{Reliability and series systems.} The law $1-\prod(1-\pow_i)$ is
formally the probability that at least one of $n$ independent events occurs,
and appears in reliability theory. We do not claim the powers are
probabilities; they are fractions of a gap, and the law is derived in
\cref{thm:multiplicative} from the recurrence rather than assumed from an
independence hypothesis.

\paragraph{Provenance monoids.} The composition of provenance tags under
$\max$ over an ordered three-element set is an idempotent commutative monoid
with an absorbing element. Ladder composition is the same shape without
idempotence: identity $0$, absorbing element $1$, commutative, associative.
The parallel is worth stating because the accompanying results---composition
is a fold, a chain cannot regress, and the aggregate inherits monotonicity only
under a stated hypothesis---transfer directly, and because it locates what is
distinctive here as the loss of idempotence rather than the algebra.

\paragraph{Screens versus identity tests.} A construct that assigns each item
an invariant, and identifies items whose invariants agree, is a screen and not
an identity test: agreement is necessary for sameness and not sufficient.
\Cref{prop:converse-note} states this for ladders explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[A ladder is a screen, not an identity test]
\label{prop:converse-note}
Equality of composite power does not entail equality of power sequences.
Ladders with different power sequences may share a composite.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(0.5,0.5)$ and $(0.75)$ both give composite $0.75$ by \cref{eq:compose}. The
full power sequences differ, and the intermediate gaps differ, so an observer
recording the whole trajectory distinguishes them; an observer recording only
the composite does not.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the limitation is stated rather than buried]
\label{rem:screen}
\Cref{prop:converse-note} is the natural objection to any claim that a ladder
characterises a process, and it is correct. An experiment resolving only
end-to-end efficiency underdetermines the ladder. What the ladder determines is
what an observer of the whole trajectory sees, and \cref{sec:results} reports
the trajectory rather than the summary wherever both are available.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.4em]

\item Everything downstream of \cref{lem:floor} requires the contact graph to
be finite (\cref{rem:finiteness}). For systems admitting unlimited refinement
no uniform floor exists and the results do not apply as stated.

\item Order independence is graded, not exact (\cref{prop:graded}). We measured
the reduction on one family of instances---chains of six items---and the
numbers in \cref{tab:order} are properties of that family. We claim the
ordering of the counts, not their values.

\item \Cref{thm:intensive} guarantees invariance only against modifications
outside the ball. Commitment reaches inside neighbouring balls, which is
exactly why \cref{prop:graded} is graded, and we have not characterised which
mutation disciplines would restore exactness.

\item Powers are derived from a contact graph, not from chemical structure. The
graph is itself a model, and the suite records as \textsc{not tested} the
question of whether a derived power matches any measured rate.

\item The ladder inherits the reversal limitation
(\cref{cor:reversal-transfer}): it cannot orient a process, and orientation
remains a question for the medium.

\item \Cref{prop:conservative} establishes conservativity syntactically---no
existing rule is modified and added rules apply only to added syntax. It does
not establish that no existing program's \emph{meaning} changes under a reading
that mixes the two composition rules, and we have not investigated such
readings.

\item The exhaustive minimisation in \cref{eq:local-sepcost} is exponential in
the ball size. This bounds the radius at which the construction is practical
and is why the measurements use small graphs. A maximum-flow implementation
would remove the limit and we have not written one.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Shakespeare had one composition rule, and it was extensive: residue chaining
adds the boundary a process commits, is invariant under reordering, and cannot
say how much of a process any step accomplishes. We added a second rule that is
intensive, and the two are independent: neither aggregate determines the other,
so keeping both is not redundancy.

Three things were harder than expected and are reported as they came out.
Intensivity is not delivered by a local cut key alone---the normaliser must be
local too, and the near-miss control is what localised the failure. Order
independence is graded rather than exact, because commitment reaches inside
neighbouring balls, which is precisely the case the invariance theorem does not
cover. And the radius sweep runs in the direction opposite to the one we
predicted, for a reason---ball overlap---that we report beside the counts so
the direction is explained rather than merely recorded.

The construct earns its place by refusing. A ladder whose declared rungs cannot
reach its declared target is rejected before evaluation, the rejection is tight
in the sense that it excludes nothing that could have succeeded, and it is
non-vacuous in the sense that presentable ladders fall on both sides of it. A
construct that always succeeded would classify nothing, and the framework says
so.

Two published claims are falsified by this extension and both are amended in
place: there are now two composition rules rather than one, and freeness is a
criterion rather than a property of a single privileged rule. The second
amendment is the more useful, because \cref{thm:free} replaces a licence
granted case by case with a test any rule may be put to.
