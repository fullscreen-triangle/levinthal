One chain of reasoning, five links. 01

Individuation is not free — so there is a floor.

Telling one thing apart from its surroundings costs a strictly positive minimum. In a finite weighted contact graph that minimum is just the least edge weight, and it is positive because a finite set of positive numbers has a positive minimum. Nothing here is posited; the floor is computed. Take away finiteness and it vanishes.

β = mine∈E w(e) > 0

02

Probing is free; committing is floored. That asymmetry is catalysis.

Entering a region costs nothing. Individuating it — drawing the boundary — costs at least β, whatever the region's rarity. So a structure committed once can be traversed indefinitely without paying again. An enzyme is reusable for that reason, not an energetic one, and this is what an account of catalysis has to explain first.

cost(probe) = 0  ·  cost(commit) ≥ β

03

A process is a ladder: one number per step, composing multiplicatively.

Drop the participants. Each step carries exactly one datum — its power, the fraction of the remaining gap it closes — and a process is a sequence of them. Because each closes a fraction of what remains, the residuals multiply. Two steps of equal power are the same rung: a rung has no identity field, and that is the formalism rather than an omission.

π(L) = 1 − ∏i(1 − πi)

1 · Why enzymes exist

The usual answer — enzymes lower an activation barrier — describes what a catalyst does to a rate. It does not say why the catalyst survives the reaction it catalyses, and reusability is the property that actually needs explaining.

Start further back. Existence is by distinction, and distinction takes time, so individuating a part of a bounded whole costs a strictly positive minimum: the floor β. In a finite weighted contact graph with a medium vertex adjacent to every item, β is the least edge weight, and it is positive for one reason — a finite set of strictly positive numbers has a positive minimum.

The hypothesis is load-bearing and easy to check: the weights 1/n are each positive and admit no positive lower bound, so a system permitted to refine without limit has no floor at all. The cell below computes β on real graphs and exhibits that failure branch beside it.

The asymmetry is the whole account. Probing a region draws no boundary and costs nothing. Committing one costs at least β regardless of how improbable the region is. So a structure committed once is thereafter traversed for free — which is reusability, derived rather than assumed, and with no energetic argument anywhere in it.

Resolution, not threshold

A rung's power is derived from the separation cost against the medium, computed on a ball of declared radius. The radius is a resolution parameter, not a tunable threshold: its effect is monotone and its direction is a stated consequence rather than a fitted one.

We predicted the direction backwards. The expectation was that a coarse radius would identify more items. It is the reverse, and the mechanism is ball overlap: at radius 0 the balls are disjoint singletons so every item gets its own value; as the radius grows the balls coincide and distinct items minimise over the same edges. The cell reports the overlap beside the count, because a count without its mechanism would leave the direction unexplained.

2 · The process ladder

A process is usually described by naming its participants and ordering them. A ladder describes the same process without any of that: each step carries one number, its power π ∈ [0,1], and the composite is 1 − ∏(1−πi).

Set the powers below and watch the gap close. Two quantities matter: the composite, which is what the ladder achieves end to end, and the sensitivity ∂π(L)/∂πj = P/(1−πj), which says where a marginal improvement pays.

Control sits at the strongest rung, not the weakest. The quantity being maximised is transmission, not slack: a gain at rung j reaches the output only through ∏i≠j(1−πi), so improving a weak rung buys a large local change the rest of the ladder then discards. Is the law right?

The multiplicative law is scored against additive, maximum and mean on the same generated ladders. Scoring only the favoured law would not distinguish it from the others, so all four are scored and the comparison is the test.

Intensivity, and the control that localises the failure

The multiplicative law presupposes that a rung's power does not depend on what else is in the graph. That is a requirement, and it is tested rather than assumed — it fails for the obvious construction, where a local cut key is normalised by the global floor.

Shakespeare is a receiver language whose state is the contact graph and whose clock M counts committed cuts. Evaluation is measurement: performing a play is performing the measurements it names, so re-running one is a new commitment at a higher M and never a cached retrieval.

5 · Federated querying, and the refusal that fits

The last question is whether the construct fits — whether it can be added to a query language built by other hands without breaking what is there. HFQ has six verdicts applied in a fixed order, a capability check that runs before any request is issued, and a water-filling allocator.

A ladder step is local: it reaches no source, issues no request, demands no capability. It needed no change to the capability checker or the allocator, because both guard on is this a source access rather than enumerating the local kinds they knew about. The host's own suite of sixteen checks reads 16/16 before the change and 16/16 after.

The refusal reuses the host's blame discipline. A ladder that cannot reach its target reports starved with the predecessor field empty — the input arrived intact and answered correctly, so naming it would accuse a step that did nothing wrong and send the blame walk one hop too far.

A bound the host already had

HFQ bounds retention below by the union bound ρ ≥ 1 − Σ(1−rᵢ) — the additive relaxation of the ladder's law. Measured on the same numbers, the additive form goes vacuous on most chains of four stages or more, while the multiplicative form stays informative. The two lines of work were not written with each other in view and they meet at 1 − ∏(1−xᵢ).

6 · The corpus

Two public services with different schemas, fetched once and then frozen. The host forbids network access in its adapters by construction — its claims are properties of the compiler, and a live service can neither confirm nor refute them — so the data is real but no result depends on a service being reachable.

What went wrong, and stayed in the record

Three findings came out against expectation and are reported as they came out.

  • Intensivity needed a local normaliser, not just a local cut key. The first construction used the global floor and failed.
  • Order independence is graded, not exact. We set out to prove exactness; commitment reaches inside neighbouring balls, which is exactly the case the invariance theorem does not cover.
  • The radius sweep runs backwards from the predicted direction, for a reason — ball overlap — reported beside the counts.

Two defects surfaced while building. A regular expression read a declared target as an extra rung, because power occurs inside expect power — a plan would have composed a ladder nobody wrote. And a corpus field came back empty on first extraction; querying the live service rather than recording a plausible zero is what caught it.
