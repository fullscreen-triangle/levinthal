\chapter*{Foreword}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Foreword}
\markboth{Foreword}{Foreword}
\pagestyle{fancy}

\noindent Every field of science passes through a phase in which the correct
explanations are known but the unifying principle is not.  The explanations
accumulate in separate frameworks, each internally consistent, none
derivable from the others.  The phase ends when someone identifies the
common foundation and shows that the frameworks are corollaries.

Biochemistry has been in that phase for decades.  Transition-state theory
explains enzyme catalysis.  Marcus theory explains electron transfer.
Flux-balance analysis explains metabolic flow.  Population genetics explains
pharmacogenomic variation.  Each framework is correct within its domain.
None is derivable from the others.

The common foundation is \emph{bounded phase space}.  Physical systems
occupy finite regions of phase space.  Finite regions admit partition.
Partitions admit nesting.  Nested partitions define a depth coordinate.
Depth has cost.  Cost creates gradient.  Gradient creates dynamics.  This
chain is analytic, not empirical, and it is closed: no additional assumptions
are required.

This monograph executes the programme for cytochrome~P450, the most
pharmacologically consequential enzyme family in human biology.  It does not
apply the partition framework to one reaction or one isoform.  It applies it
to everything: all 57 human isoforms, all major reaction classes, all
spectroscopic observables, all pharmacogenomic outcomes, the complete electron
transfer chain at femtosecond resolution, and the complete catalytic cycle as
a topological orbit.

Seventeen papers are assembled here.  The foundational paper establishes the
partition framework across five biological domains.  The prefatory paper
(the introduction) explains why cytochrome~P450 is the right test system and
why the framework replaces, rather than supplements, every competing model.
Papers~1--15 execute the programme systematically.  Every claim is
accompanied by a validation script; 132 scripts pass.

The deepest result is not any single rate constant or spectroscopic
prediction.  It is the structural fact that the protein does not search.
Levinthal's conformational space of $3^{503} \approx 10^{240}$ alternatives
is not a space the protein samples.  It is a mathematical artefact that arises
when one forces the partition framework's evaluation into the language of
combinatorial search.  The protein evaluates its receiver.  The native fold
is what the evaluation returns.  The conformational space was never traversed.
It was never consulted.  It was, in a precise sense, never there.

That is the message of this monograph.  The rest is the demonstration.

\chapter*{Preface}

\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

\noindent\textbf{What this book is.}
This volume assembles the complete Levinthal monograph on cytochrome~P450 in
the order the ideas were developed: first the general framework (the
biological-partition-landscape paper), then the stage-setting introduction,
then fifteen application papers working from the abstract receiver construction
through to pharmacogenomic prediction.

\noindent\textbf{What this book is not.}
It is not a textbook of P450 biochemistry.  Readers seeking background on the
P450 superfamily should consult the Guengerich review~\citep{guengerich2015}
or the Denisov structural review~\citep{denisov2005}.  This volume assumes
familiarity with the catalytic cycle, basic enzyme kinetics, and undergraduate
quantum mechanics.

\noindent\textbf{How to read it.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item For the philosophical argument: Foreword + Prefatory Paper (Chapter~0).
  \item For the mathematical framework alone: Chapter~0, §§2--5 and
    Chapter~1 (the BPL paper).
  \item For the specific P450 chemistry: Parts~II--VII.
  \item For validation only: the back-matter table, or the
    \texttt{validation/} directory of each paper.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Two universal constants.}
The entire monograph runs on two empirically calibrated numbers:
$\nufloor = 10^{10}$~s$^{-1}$ and $\Tpart = 65$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$.
They are set in the BPL paper and never adjusted.

\noindent\textbf{Notation.}
$\Depth$ and $\Delta M$ are interchangeable (partition depth / activation
partition depth).  $\dC$ is the categorical distance (number of partition
boundaries).  The rate law is $k = \nufloor\,\exp(-\Delta M)$.

\noindent\textbf{Compiling the book.}
Each paper must be compiled individually before the master document.  Run
\texttt{make all} from the \texttt{publication/} directory using the
provided \texttt{Makefile}, or compile each paper manually in the order
listed in Chapter~0, §8.

\noindent Levinthal's paradox is taught with a popular illustration: a monkey
at a typewriter, given infinite time, eventually produces \textit{Hamlet}.
The implication is that the protein's success is the kind of vanishingly rare
event that random sampling could in principle achieve.  The implication is
wrong on every count, and the wrongness is computable.

\medskip
\noindent This monograph is the consequence of taking that computation
seriously.  Cytochrome~P450 does not search among $3^{503} \approx 10^{240}$
conformations.  It evaluates a receiver.  The native fold, the catalytic
cycle, the electron transfer chain, the substrate specificity, the
pharmacogenomic consequences---all of these are what the evaluation returns.

\medskip
\noindent The derivation proceeds from a single geometric principle:
\emph{bounded phase space admits partition and nesting}.  From this principle,
partition depth $\Depth$ emerges as the fundamental quantity governing all
processes.  The rate law $k = \nufloor\,\exp(-\Delta\Depth)$ with
$\nufloor = 10^{10}$~s$^{-1}$ and partition temperature
$\Tpart = 65$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ governs every rate in the monograph.  The
categorical efficiency relation $\log_{10}(\kcat/\KM) \approx 10 - \dC$
governs every selectivity.  The compound phenotype formula
$\Delta\Depth_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Delta\Depth_{\mathrm{allele}} +
\sum_j \ln(1 + [I_j]/K_{i,j})$ governs every pharmacogenomic prediction.

\medskip
\noindent Seventeen papers are collected here: the foundational
biological-partition-landscape paper, the prefatory introduction, and fifteen
application papers covering the P450 receiver construction, sequence-manifold
geometry, equilibrium states, the five-layer electron transfer observation,
Compound~I, C--H activation, heteroatom reactions, atypical reactions, all 57
human isoforms, pharmacogenomics, membrane anchoring, the seven-state closed
orbit, spectroscopy, database recovery, and polymorphism/DDI prediction.
All~132 validation scripts pass.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cytochrome P450, partition depth, categorical
mechanics, bounded phase space, receiver evaluation, Levinthal paradox,
electron transfer, pharmacogenomics, partition gradient flow

\chaptermark{Biological Partition Landscape}

\begin{chapterabstract}
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
We derive a theory of biological dynamics from a single geometric principle:
physical systems occupy bounded regions of phase space that admit partition and
nesting.  From this principle, we establish partition depth $\Depth$ as the
fundamental quantity governing all processes.  We prove: (i)~any process
requires partitioning participants from the totality of reality, incurring an
unavoidable existence cost; (ii)~dynamics flow along partition depth gradients;
(iii)~catalysts function by providing alternative partition topologies with
reduced depth barriers; (iv)~living systems are self-maintaining partition
structures.  Applied to enzyme catalysis, we derive reaction rates, substrate
specificity, and catalytic efficiency from partition geometry alone.  Applied
to metabolism, we derive pathway architecture and flux distributions.  Applied
to disease, we derive pathology as partition malformation.  All results emerge
as corollaries of the central theorem with zero free parameters.  We validate
predictions against enzyme kinetic data spanning six orders of magnitude in
catalytic efficiency, metabolic flux measurements, and disease progression
correlations.
\end{chapterabstract}

\noindent This book is compiled in two stages.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: Individual papers.}
Each paper in \texttt{cytochrome/publications/} and the foundational paper in
\texttt{publication/biological-partition-landscape/} must be compiled to PDF
before the master document.  The provided \texttt{Makefile} (in this directory)
compiles all papers in dependency order:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{cd publication/ \&\& make all}
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Stage 2: Master document.}
After all paper PDFs exist:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{cd publication/ \&\& make book}
\end{quote}
or
\begin{quote}
\texttt{pdflatex cytochrome-p450-monograph.tex \&\& \\
pdflatex cytochrome-p450-monograph.tex}
\end{quote}
(Two passes are needed for correct TOC and cross-references.)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Missing PDFs.}
If a paper PDF is absent, the corresponding chapter will display a grey stub
box with the compilation instruction rather than failing.  The book is
therefore browsable at any stage of compilation.
